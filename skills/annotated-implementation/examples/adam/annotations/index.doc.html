Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization

This is a PyTorch implementation of Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization (Kingma and Ba, 2015), with the derivations behind each line of the update.

Stochastic gradient descent takes a step of the same size, in every direction, for every parameter. That is a poor fit when gradients for different parameters live on very different scales, or when some parameters only get a useful gradient once in a while. AdaGrad and RMSProp fix this by dividing each parameter's gradient by a running estimate of its magnitude. Adam keeps a running average of the gradient (momentum) and of the squared gradient, divides one by the square root of the other, and corrects both for being started at zero. The result is a step whose size in parameter space is roughly the learning rate $\alpha$, whatever the scale of the gradients.

  • Adam - the optimizer, why the moving averages need bias correction, and how big its steps can get.
  • AdaMax - replacing the squared-gradient average with an exponentially weighted infinity norm, derived as the limit of an $L^p$ norm.
  • Convergence - the paper's regret bound, a sketch of its proof, and why the proof does not hold for Adam as written.
